Virtual business address for your US LLC

A real commercial street address with your own suite number, in Wyoming, Delaware, Florida, Texas, California or New York. Your mail is received by a USPS-registered mailroom and scanned the same day, so you can run a US company from anywhere.

What a virtual business address is

A virtual business address is a street address at a commercial building that you can use as your company's official mailing and business address without renting desk space. The building is operated by a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) registered with the US Postal Service, and you get a unique suite or unit number that identifies your mail.

Because it is a real street address, not a P.O. Box, it can be used in places where a P.O. Box is refused, such as many state business filings and bank account applications.

Where you can use it

  • State filings — the principal office or mailing address on your LLC's Articles of Organization and annual reports
  • IRS forms — the mailing address on Form SS-4 when you apply for an EIN, and on later tax filings
  • Banking and payments — the business address on applications to fintech banks and payment processors such as Mercury, Relay, Brex and Stripe (each provider makes its own approval decision)
  • Your website, invoices, contracts and marketplace seller accounts
  • Business cards, letterheads and email signatures

One thing to know: Google Business Profile guidelines generally do not allow virtual offices or mailboxes to be listed as a storefront location unless the address is staffed during business hours. Use your virtual address for filings and correspondence, not as a Maps listing.

Is a virtual address allowed for an LLC?

Yes. US states do not require an LLC to lease office space. What every state does require is a registered agent with a physical address in the state of formation, available during normal business hours to accept legal papers. That role is separate from your business address — see our registered agent service.
