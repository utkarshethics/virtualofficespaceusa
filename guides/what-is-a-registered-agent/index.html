What is a registered agent?

A registered agent is the person or company your LLC or corporation names to receive lawsuits and official state mail. Every state requires one. Here is what the role involves and how to choose one.

Updated October 2026

The short answer

A registered agent is your company's official point of contact with the state. They accept service of process (notice of a lawsuit), subpoenas and official notices from the Secretary of State and state tax authorities, then pass them to you. Some states call it a resident agent, statutory agent or agent for service of process — it is the same role.

What the law requires

  • A physical street address in the state where your company is formed or registered — no P.O. Boxes
  • Someone available at that address during normal business hours
  • The agent's consent to serve
  • An agent for as long as the company exists, and in every state where it is registered

Can you be your own registered agent?

Yes, if you live or have an office in the state and can be there during business hours. The trade-offs: your address goes on the public record, you have to be available every business day, and you cannot serve as agent in a state where you have no address. That is why non-residents, remote founders and anyone who values privacy use a registered agent service.

What happens without a registered agent

  • You can miss a lawsuit and have a default judgment entered against your company
  • You can miss state notices and annual report deadlines
  • The state can mark the company as not in good standing and eventually dissolve it

Registered agent vs. business address

The registered agent receives legal papers. Your business address receives everything else — customer mail, bank letters and IRS correspondence — and is what you put on your website and invoices. Many companies use one provider for both.
